# Resume Builder: Frameworks & Deep Material

Pull-on-demand depth for the `resume-builder` skill, distilled from the four-leader synthesis. Attribution is kept because the named frameworks belong to specific people, and knowing the source helps judge edge cases.

## Bullet formulas

**XYZ (Google's formula; taught verbatim by Su and Belcak).**
`Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z].`
The result comes first, the metric proves it, the method explains it. Belcak's example: "Increased QoQ sales 17% by designing new conversion-focused Facebook ad imagery." Su's: "Drove 2,500 organic signups to our newsletter by A/B testing content layout, a 43% quarter-on-quarter increase."

**Effect-then-cause (LaCivita).** Same elements, ordered as outcome first, then what you did to cause it. Structurally compatible with XYZ; use whichever reads cleaner for a given bullet.

**Belcak's 4-Bucket Anatomy.** Almost every word in a strong bullet falls into one of four buckets: (1) hard & soft skills, (2) common connective words, (3) action / power / emotional words, (4) measurable metrics. If a bullet is all bucket-2, it's empty. Score a bullet by checking it has something from buckets 1, 3, and 4.

## Quantifying when there's no obvious number

All the leaders agree you can almost always find a number; they teach different levers. Use whichever fits.

- **The "so what?" ladder (Su).** Ask "so what?" repeatedly until you reach an outcome or a number. Stop at the first genuinely impressive rung.
- **Benchmark against a baseline (Su).** "Scored 23% above the class average." A relative number works when an absolute one doesn't exist.
- **Roll up / aggregate (LaCivita).** Sum repetitions across time to manufacture heft: a hostess "handled 25,000 customers per year" (100/day × 5 days × 50 weeks). "Mileage, not calendar time."
- **Deductive estimation (LaCivita).** Build a defensible figure from known inputs: a two-week acceleration on $144M annual revenue ≈ $6M cash-flow impact. Estimate openly; don't fabricate precision.
- **Belcak's six levers.** Scope, efficiency, time, quantity, comparison, and leveraging others (ask the team that consumed your work how it performed — e.g., a designer asks sales how the deck converted).
- **Impact, not complexity (LaCivita).** Hide the trivial tool (an Excel macro, a DocuSign setup); show the dollar effect it produced.

Guardrail shared by all: don't lie. The effort to quantify matters more to a recruiter than the exact digits.

## Top-of-resume summary (default: bulleted highlights)

The skill's default is Belcak's bulleted objective: 3–5 bullets, ≤50 words each, employer-aligned, most relevant first. A workable structure: bullet 1 = background + top hard skills + years; bullets 2–3 = mini case studies with metrics; bullet 4 = cultural/soft fit. It's the hook that earns the rest of the read.

**Alternative: LaCivita's career profile (paragraph).** 1–2 short paragraphs: who you are, what you've accomplished (aggregated lifetime numbers), and a one-line skills summary. Use this for senior/executive resumes where narrative heft and anchoring matter more than skimmability. LaCivita's compression test: "28 years of experience stripped down to 26 words."

Both camps agree the top third is decisive and must be about the employer, not the candidate. They only disagree on format, which is why the skill picks one default and keeps the other as an escape hatch.

## Keyword extraction & tailoring

The findability process (Su + Belcak, reconciled with Mann's "no fluff" guardrail):

1. Collect the target job description(s). For a class of similar roles, paste several JDs into one doc.
2. Extract the recurring terms by word-cloud, or an AI prompt like: *"Analyze these job descriptions and list the top 10 keywords by frequency, showing the count for each."*
3. Keep only the terms the person honestly has. Drop generic fluff the tool suggests ("collaborative", "team player"); recruiters don't search those.
4. Place each kept keyword where it's honest: the highlights, the skills section, and at least one experience bullet. Mirror the JD's exact phrasing where natural (recruiters paste requirements; they don't type synonyms).
5. Target roughly the same density of the JD's core terms: enough to be findable, never stuffed past the length ceiling.

**Master-resume pattern (Su / LaCivita in spirit).** Keep one foundational resume holding everything. Tailor per application by swapping/reordering highlight bullets and keyword phrasing, ~15 minutes, not a rewrite. Apply early; speed beats a late-but-perfect submission.

## Named frameworks worth knowing (single-source, kept for depth)

- **LaCivita: Career Profile + aggregation ("rolling up") + "the three home runs"** (generate revenue, save costs, optimize the foundation). His resume goal: "entice an employer to speak with you," nothing more. He rejects ATS optimization ("applicant trashing systems") in favor of "boss hunting / teammate hunting" — putting the resume directly in a human's hands. The skill keeps his bypass instinct as a *complement* to networking, not as a reason to skip findability.
- **Belcak: Value Validation Project (VVP).** A deliverable that solves a real need for the target employer (a case study, teardown, slide deck, or repo), hosted online and linked under the matching skill. His marquee differentiator; strongest for technical/creative roles. Also his four format types: Chronological (cap ~10–15 years), Functional, Combination, Creative.
- **Mann: GLORY Formula.** Named in the corpus but the acronym is never decoded (she gates the full version behind a masterclass). The method recoverable from her material: drop "autobiography syndrome," start from the company's needs, use the JD's real keywords, build the resume backward from what they want. Her sharpest line: prove the trait with a vivid story, don't claim it with an adjective.
- **Su: the "so what?" ladder + word-cloud keyword method + data backing** (Belcak's 125,484-resume study: the 475–600-word sweet spot, 51% average keyword match, metrics in only 26% of resumes).

## Action verbs (the corpus is thin here: use this starter bank)

The leaders don't supply a full verb list, so this is assembled from their scattered examples plus standard practice. Lead bullets with these, never with "responsible for."

Built, launched, shipped, drove, grew, cut, reduced, increased, automated, consolidated, redesigned, negotiated, led, scaled, re-engineered, pioneered, founded, delivered, accelerated, streamlined, recovered, secured, migrated.

## Anti-patterns (kill on sight)

- "Responsible for", "duties included", "tasked with": describes a role, not an accomplishment.
- Bullets a same-title peer could copy verbatim (that's a job description).
- Buzzwords and cliches: "detail-oriented", "team player", "results-driven", "synergy".
- Skill bar-graphs / self-rated proficiency charts (subjective; ATS can't read them).
- Hidden or white-text keywords (stripped to plain text and flagged; reads as dishonest).
- Creative section headers ("My Journey", "Where I've Made Impact"): parsers miscategorize them.
- Contact info in the header/footer (commonly dropped by parsers).
- A photo, in most markets (high discard rate; varies by country/role).

## Worked before/after (pattern reference)

- Activity → impact: "Assisted with project governance and presented to a banking client" → "Helped a banking client successfully open a local-market branch by leading project governance and client presentations."
- Vague → quantified: "Managed social media accounts" → "Grew social media engagement 30% in six months by launching a new content strategy."
- The extreme case (shows the principle): even "Fetched coffee for the team" becomes "Delivered ~9 coffee orders daily at 100% accuracy across a 3-month internship" — scope and accuracy beat a bare task. (Use judgment; don't pad trivial work onto a senior resume.)
