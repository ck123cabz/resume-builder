# Assessments: take-home tests

Some applications include a test: grammar multiple choice, or writing replies to sample
customers. Treat each item as the employer grading the person, and answer for that grader.

## Before answering

1. **Name what the item tests.** For a customer reply that is usually: did they read every fact
   the customer gave, did they stay calm, did they solve it in one message, and did they avoid
   promising what the company may not do.
2. **Research the company's public policy.** Search its help center for the topic (repayment
   dates, card linking, refunds). Use only what the public pages say, and cite them to the person.
   When a page does not cover it, say what you assumed.
3. **List every fact in the prompt.** Each one the customer stated must be answered or used.
   Asking the customer for a fact they already gave reads as not reading the message.

## Multiple choice

Give the answer, then one line per rejected option naming its error ("missing comma after the
opening clause", "'cases' should be singular").

## Written replies

- **Voice:** the way the role talks to customers. For support, polite and plain: thank them, apologize for
  the experience, say what is fixed, say what they do if it happens again, close warmly.
- **Commit only to what policy allows.** A date change the help center says support can make is
  fine; a refund or a process change it does not mention is an assumption, so flag it.
- **When the person asks for "what we'll do so it doesn't happen again"**, add one concrete step
  within policy (a note on the account, the message passed to the team) and flag it as assumed.
- **Short beats complete.** Lead with the fix; keep it to what the customer needs today.
- Expect revisions: the person may ask for "customer service mode", "simpler", or "ask for more
  info". Each is a full rewrite in that direction, and the answer the grader rewards may differ
  from the first instinct. Say so when a direction would cost points, and why.

## Before handing over

Run the linter if available, strip markdown for the form field, and list every assumption about
company policy or the person's authority under the answer.
